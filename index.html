Asia ROAS Multiplier Model

Directional spend-weighting by age and gender across three growth markets, plus a future-demand uplift on the 18–25 recruitment cohort.

Same logic in every tab — a segment multiplier indexed off Brand Equity Index (BEI), with a +30% uplift on 18–25 as a future-demand adjustment. All three markets have now fielded their own Wave 2 Brand Health Tracker (NIQ, fielded 17–29 June 2026) with BEI broken out by age, gender and household income, so every tab below indexes off that market’s own measured data — nothing here is carried across from another market.

Taiwan’s report is still marked Preliminary (V1) as of 17 Aug 2026, so treat that tab as provisional pending the final cut.

Reading this responsibly
  • BEI is a proxy, not ROAS. It's the closest thing Wave 2 has to a purchase-propensity signal (favourite brand %, recommend %, willingness to pay premium), but it's brand sentiment, not measured media efficiency.
  • The combined grid assumes independence. Age and gender multipliers are multiplied together because the tracker reports them as separate marginals — there's no actual "Female 18–25" BEI cell in the data.
  • The +30% sits on 18–25 only. That's the one cohort the Brand Blueprint explicitly names as needing investment now for growth later ("attracting younger consumers... is essential for sustainable growth"). 26–35 is already the stated core target, not a recruitment segment, so it wasn't extended there.
  • Household income isn't in this grid. Wave 2 also reports BEI by income (High 5.0 / Medium 4.4 / Low 3.4) — adding it as a third dimension would sharpen this further, but could pull the 46–60/Female weighting up or down depending on how income correlates with that age band, which the tracker doesn't show directly.

Reading this responsibly
  • This tab is still Preliminary (V1). Taiwan's Wave 2 report is dated 25 Aug 2026 and marked preliminary on its cover — treat these multipliers as provisional and re-check against the final report before locking budget splits.
  • 26–35 is unusually dominant here. At BEI 3.3 against a total of just 2.5, it's Taiwan's only strong segment (26–35 Female indexes at 1.43x, even before the future-demand uplift) — every other age band sits at the same 2.2 BEI, which is why 18–25, 36–45 and 46–60 all carry identical age multipliers.
